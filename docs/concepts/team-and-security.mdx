Everyone in Consultainer belongs to an **organization** with one of three
roles: **Owner**, **Admin** or **Member**. This page covers what each role
can do, how people join, and how to secure sign-in with single sign-on
(SSO), two-factor authentication and passkeys.

Over the [REST API](/api-reference/introduction) and [MCP server](/mcp-server)
a key acts as the person who made it, with their role, with some extra
limits: generating invoices (which members can do in the app), emailing
invoices, marking invoices sent or paid, voiding them, approving expenses, reading pending timesheets and reading the
audit log need an Admin or Owner key. A member's key also never sees the
client or project `shareToken` (the secret in client links).

[Client documents](/concepts/clients-and-projects) have their own access
setting: **Everyone**, **Owners & admins**, or **Specific people**. The Owner
and whoever uploaded a document can always open it.

### The owner

The person who completes `/setup` becomes the organization's **Owner**. There
is one owner, and the role can't be granted, changed or removed in the app
(invites and SSO only create Admins and Members, and **Make admin** / **Make
member** never grant Owner). The server enforces this too: admins can't
change or remove an owner, and an organization's last owner can't be demoted
or removed (*An organization needs at least one owner.*). The owner:

- always keeps password sign-in, so a broken identity provider can't lock
  everyone out;
- receives alert emails when **Settings → Alerts** has no email list;
- is the only person who can change instance-wide settings, because they
  affect every organization on the server.

## Invite people

Who can do it: **Owner**, **Admin**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Members">
    Go to **Settings → Members** and click **Invite member**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter their email and role">
    Type their email address, pick **Member** or **Admin** under **Role**, and
    click **Create invite**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get the link to them">
    If [email](/self-hosting#email) is set up, they're emailed an invite.
    Either way the dialog shows the invite link with a **Copy** button, so you
    can send it yourself. The link expires in 14 days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They accept">
    The link opens **Join &lt;your organization&gt;**. New people set their name
    and a password (at least 10 characters, with a letter and a number).
    Someone who already has an account on this server just accepts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Check it worked:** until it's used, the invite is listed under **Pending
invites** on **Settings → Members**, with a copy button for the link and an
**×** to revoke it. Once accepted, the person appears under **Members** and
owners and admins get a notification.

There's no public sign-up. Besides invites, people can join through
[SSO with automatic account creation](#single-sign-on-sso) or
[auto-join by email domain](#auto-join-by-email-domain).

## Manage members

Who can do it: **Owner**, **Admin**. On **Settings → Members**, open the
**⋯** menu next to a person to:

- **Make admin** / **Make member**: change their role.
- **Mark as contractor** / **Mark as employee**: contractors get a
  **Contractor** badge. Contractors can be asked to confirm their rate on a
  project, and **Timesheet approval → Contractors** (Settings → General)
  applies only to them.
- **Create password reset link**: makes a one-time link for them to choose
  a new password. See [Password resets](#password-resets).
- **Remove**: takes them out of the organization. Their time entries and
  other work stay. Their API keys stop working for this organization.

The owner's row has no menu for admins. Another owner sees just **Create
password reset link** there. Your own row has no menu: change your own
password on **Profile**.

Owners and admins also see a **No 2FA** badge next to anyone who hasn't set
up an authenticator app, so you can ask them to. It turns red while
[Require two-factor authentication](#require-two-factor-authentication) is
on. People who have to sign in with SSO don't get the badge.

### Default bill rates

Each member row shows a **Rate** field: what that person bills per hour by
default, in your organization's default currency.

1. Go to **Settings → Members**.
2. Type a number in **Rate** next to the person and press Enter or click
   away to save.

When someone is added to a project, their rate there starts at this number
(you can still change it in the **Add team member** dialog). Leave it empty
to use the organization's **Default bill rate** (Settings → General), shown
greyed out in the empty field; with neither set, people start at 0. Changing
it never changes their rate on projects they're already on: use **Edit
rate** on the project for that. Changes trigger the **Billing or payment
details change** alert.

### Cost rates

Each member row shows a **Cost** field: what an hour of that person's time
costs you (salary or contractor rate), per hour. Type a number and press
Enter or click away to save. Leave it empty if you don't track it.

Default bill rates and cost rates are only shown to owners and admins. [Reports](/concepts/reports)
use them, together with **Overhead (%)** from Settings → General, to work out
profit per project. They never appear on invoices; what clients pay comes
from each person's bill rate on the project.

## Password resets

There are two ways to reset a forgotten password: the person asks for an
email themselves, or an owner or admin creates a link and hands it over. The
second also works on a server without [email](/integrations/email).

### Reset your own password

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a link">
    On the login page, click **Forgot password?** under the password box,
    enter your email address, and click **Email me a reset link**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the email">
    You see *If an account exists for that email, we've sent a link to reset
    your password.* whether or not there is one. Open the **Reset your
    Consultainer password** email and click **Choose a new password**. The
    link works once and expires in 1 hour.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a new password">
    Enter it twice (at least 10 characters, with a letter and a number) and
    click **Set new password**, then **Log in**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the server can't send email, **Forgot password?** says so and tells you
to ask an owner or admin for a link instead.

### Create a reset link for someone

Who can do it: **Owner**, **Admin** (admins can't do it for owners).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the menu">
    Go to **Settings → Members** and open the **⋯** menu next to the person.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the link">
    Click **Create password reset link**. A dialog shows the link with a
    **Copy** button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it to them">
    Send it yourself, by chat or in person. It isn't emailed. It works once
    and expires in 24 hours.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Check it worked:** **Settings → Audit log** shows *created a password reset
link for* the person, and later *reset their password* once they've used
it. Both also raise the **Access or security settings change** alert.

### What a reset changes

- **Only the newest link works.** Asking for or creating a new link cancels
  any older unused one.
- **Everyone is signed out.** The person's other sessions, on every device,
  end straight away, and unused login links stop working.
- **Two-factor authentication stays on.** A reset never removes the
  authenticator app or passkeys, so the next password login still asks for
  a code. Someone who has lost their authenticator too uses a recovery
  code, or see [Self-hosting troubleshooting](/self-hosting#troubleshooting).
- **SSO still applies.** If [Require SSO](#single-sign-on-sso) is on, people
  who have to use SSO get no reset email, and the **⋯** menu has no reset
  link for them. Reset their password in your identity provider. Owners
  keep password sign-in, so they can still reset theirs.
- **Limits.** One account gets at most 3 reset emails an hour, and one
  network address can ask 10 times an hour. Extra requests show the same
  message but send nothing.

## Auto-join by email domain

Who can do it: **Owner**, **Admin**. This only applies to **Continue with
Google** sign-in, which the server admin turns on with `AUTH_GOOGLE_ID` and
`AUTH_GOOGLE_SECRET` (see [Self-hosting](/self-hosting#optional)). For other
identity providers use [SSO](#single-sign-on-sso).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the organization domain">
    Go to **Settings → Security**. In the **Domain** card, enter your
    company's email domain (for example `acme.com`) under **Organization
    domain**. It must match the domain of your own email address, can't be a
    free email provider, and can't already belong to another organization on
    the server.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on auto-join">
    Tick **Auto-join teammates with a matching email domain** and click
    **Save changes**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Anyone who signs in with a Google account on that domain and has no account
yet joins as a **Member**, and owners and admins are notified.

## Single sign-on (SSO)

Consultainer supports single sign-on with any **OpenID Connect (OIDC)**
provider: Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Authentik, Keycloak,
Zitadel, Auth0 and others. SAML isn't supported; most providers that offer
SAML also offer OIDC.

Who can do it: **Owner**, **Admin**. Each organization has one SSO
connection.

### How it works

- Once a connection is enabled, the login page shows a **Sign in with
  &lt;Button label&gt;** button.
- Sign-ins are matched to accounts by **email address**. Someone already in
  the organization (invited, or the owner) signs in to their existing
  account.
- With **Create accounts on first sign-in** on, anyone your identity
  provider lets through (and whose email domain is allowed) gets an account
  the first time, with the role under **New accounts join as**. Owners and
  admins are notified. With it off, only people already in the organization
  can sign in with SSO.
- With **Require SSO** on, password, magic-link, passkey and Google sign-in
  are blocked for everyone in the organization **except the owner**.
- Consultainer asks for the scopes `openid email profile`, uses PKCE and a
  nonce, and sends the client secret in the token request body
  (`client_secret_post`).

### Set up SSO

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the redirect URI">
    Open **Settings → Security** on the address people will sign in from (for
    example `https://time.example.com`, not `localhost`). In the **Single
    sign-on (OIDC)** card, click **Copy** next to **Redirect URI**. It looks
    like `https://time.example.com/api/sso/callback`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create an OIDC app in your identity provider">
    Follow the steps for your provider below. You come away with an
    **issuer URL**, a **client ID** and a **client secret**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the details in Consultainer">
    Back in the **Single sign-on (OIDC)** card, fill in **Issuer URL**,
    **Client ID** and **Client secret**. Optionally set:

    - **Button label**: the name on the login button, such as `Okta`.
    - **Allowed email domains**: for example `acme.com, acme.co.uk`. Leave it
      blank to accept any verified email the provider returns.
    - **Create accounts on first sign-in** and **New accounts join as**.
    - **Trust email addresses from this provider**: leave off unless sign-in
      fails with an unverified-email error (see Troubleshooting).
    - **Require SSO**: leave off until you've tested a sign-in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect">
    Click **Connect**. Consultainer fetches
    `<issuer>/.well-known/openid-configuration` to find the provider's
    endpoints. If that fails, you'll see an error and nothing is saved.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test it">
    In a private browser window, open the login page, click **Sign in with
    …**, and sign in as a test user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Require SSO (optional)">
    Once a test sign-in works, click **Edit**, tick **Require SSO**, and
    click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Check it worked:** the card shows a summary with a green check next to the
issuer, **Enabled** ticked, and the **New users**, **Domains**,
**Unverified emails** and **Enforced** settings. Untick **Enabled** to hide
the login button without deleting the connection.

### Google Workspace

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Google Cloud console">
    Go to [console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com) with a
    Workspace admin account and create or pick a project.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure the consent screen">
    Open **APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen** (shown as **Google Auth
    Platform** in newer consoles). Set the app name and support email, and
    choose the **Internal** audience so only users in your Workspace can sign
    in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the client">
    Go to **Clients** (or **APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials
    → OAuth client ID**), choose application type **Web application**, and
    add the Consultainer redirect URI under **Authorized redirect URIs**.
    Click **Create**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the values">
    - **Issuer URL**: `https://accounts.google.com`
    - **Client ID** and **Client secret**: from the dialog Google shows after
      creating the client.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Google's issuer is the same for every Google account, including personal
  Gmail. Always fill **Allowed email domains** with your Workspace domain
  (for example `acme.com`), especially with **Create accounts on first
  sign-in** on.
</Warning>

### Microsoft Entra ID

<Steps>
  <Step title="Register an app">
    In the [Microsoft Entra admin center](https://entra.microsoft.com), go to
    **Identity → Applications → App registrations → New registration**. Give
    it a name, choose **Accounts in this organizational directory only**,
    and under **Redirect URI** pick **Web** and paste the Consultainer
    redirect URI. Click **Register**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note the IDs">
    On the app's **Overview**, copy the **Application (client) ID** and the
    **Directory (tenant) ID**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a client secret">
    Go to **Certificates & secrets → Client secrets → New client secret**,
    pick an expiry, and click **Add**. Copy the secret's **Value** (not the
    Secret ID) straight away; it's only shown once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the email claim">
    Go to **Token configuration → Add optional claim**, choose token type
    **ID**, tick **email**, and click **Add**. If asked, also tick the option
    to add the Microsoft Graph email permission. Users need an email address
    set on their Entra account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the values">
    - **Issuer URL**: `https://login.microsoftonline.com/<Directory (tenant) ID>/v2.0`
    - **Client ID**: the Application (client) ID.
    - **Client secret**: the secret's Value.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Entra client secrets expire (at most after 24 months). Put a reminder in
  your calendar to create a new one and paste it in with **Edit** before the
  old one expires, or SSO sign-in stops working.
</Note>

### Okta

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an app integration">
    In the Okta Admin Console, go to **Applications → Applications → Create
    App Integration**. Choose **OIDC - OpenID Connect** and **Web
    Application**, then click **Next**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure it">
    Name it, keep the **Authorization Code** grant type, and paste the
    Consultainer redirect URI into **Sign-in redirect URIs**. Under
    **Assignments**, choose who can use it (for example everyone, or a
    group). Click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the values">
    - **Issuer URL**: your Okta domain, for example
      `https://your-org.okta.com`. If you use a custom authorization server,
      use its issuer instead (for example
      `https://your-org.okta.com/oauth2/default`).
    - **Client ID** and **Client secret**: on the app's **General** tab under
      **Client Credentials**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Okta only lets assigned users sign in, so assignments are where you control
access.

### Other providers

Create a confidential (server-side) OIDC client with the authorization code
flow, the Consultainer redirect URI, and the scopes `openid email profile`.
Use the provider's issuer URL: the address that serves
`/.well-known/openid-configuration`. For Authentik that's the provider's
**OpenID Configuration Issuer**.

The issuer URL must start with `https://`. Plain `http://` is only accepted
for a test provider on `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]`.

### SSO troubleshooting

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| *Must start with https:// (http:// only for localhost).* under **Issuer URL** | Use the provider's `https://` issuer URL. |
| **Connect** fails with *Couldn't reach &lt;issuer&gt;/.well-known/openid-configuration.* or *Couldn't fetch OIDC discovery document (404).* | The issuer URL is wrong, or the server can't reach the provider. Open that URL in a browser; it should return JSON. |
| *OIDC discovery document is missing required endpoints.* | The URL isn't an OIDC issuer. Use the provider's issuer, not its login page. |
| The provider shows a redirect URI mismatch | Register exactly the URI shown in the card. If it starts with `http://` behind HTTPS, fix your [proxy headers](/self-hosting#put-it-behind-a-domain-with-https). |
| Login page: *SSO sign-in failed. Try again or contact your admin.* | The token exchange or ID token check failed; the app log (`docker compose logs consultainer-app`) has the reason after `[sso] Sign-in failed`. Common causes: wrong or expired client secret; the provider didn't return an email (Entra: add the **email** optional claim); the provider requires a different client authentication method than sending the secret in the request body. |
| *Your identity provider marked your email as unverified. …* | The provider sent `email_verified: false`. Tick **Trust email addresses from this provider** only if users can't change their own email in the provider. Authentik 2025.10 and later need this. |
| *Your email domain isn't allowed to sign in with this SSO provider.* | Add the domain to **Allowed email domains**, or clear the list. |
| *You don't have an account yet. Ask an admin to invite you.* | **Create accounts on first sign-in** is off and the person isn't in the organization. Invite them, or turn it on. |
| *Your organization requires signing in with SSO.* | **Require SSO** is on. Use the SSO button. The owner can still sign in with their password. |

## Two-factor authentication and passkeys

Each person sets these up for their own account, on **Profile**. Owners can
also [require two-factor authentication](#require-two-factor-authentication)
for everyone in the organization.

### Turn on two-factor authentication

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start">
    Go to **Profile**, find **Two-factor authentication**, and click **Enable
    2FA**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it to your authenticator app">
    Click **Open in authenticator app**, or type the **Manual entry key**
    into 1Password, Authy, Google Authenticator or similar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Enter the **6-digit code** the app shows and click **Verify and enable**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your recovery codes">
    Copy the recovery codes somewhere safe. Each works once in place of a
    code, and they're only shown this one time. Click **Done**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

From then on, signing in with your password or an emailed login link asks
for a code. To turn it off, enter your
password under **Confirm password** and click **Disable 2FA**.

### Require two-factor authentication

Who can do it: **Owner**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn it on for yourself first">
    Set up two-factor authentication on your own **Profile** (see above).
    Otherwise saving fails with *Set up two-factor authentication on your own
    Profile first, so turning this on can't lock you out.*
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it on for the organization">
    Go to **Settings → Security**. In the **Two-factor authentication** card,
    tick **Require two-factor authentication** and click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Chase anyone who hasn't set it up">
    The card shows how many people haven't set it up yet. On **Settings →
    Members** they have a red **No 2FA** badge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

What people without an authenticator app see:

- They can still log in, but every page takes them to **Set up two-factor
  authentication** instead. The rest of the app, including its server
  actions and downloads, stays closed until they click **Enable 2FA**,
  confirm a code and save their recovery codes. **Done** then takes them to
  the dashboard.
- If they belong to several organizations and any of them requires
  two-factor authentication, the requirement applies everywhere.
- Only an **authenticator app** counts. Passkeys don't, because password
  sign-in never asks for one, so someone with only a passkey is asked to add
  an authenticator app too.
- People who signed in with the organization's **SSO** are exempt, because
  the identity provider handles their second factor. Turn on MFA in your
  identity provider.
- **API keys keep working.** The REST API and MCP server never ask for a
  second factor. Revoke the key of anyone who shouldn't keep access.

Turning it off lifts the requirement straight away. Turning it on or off
raises the **Access or security settings change** alert.

### Passkeys

On **Profile**, add a passkey to sign in with your device's fingerprint, face
or PIN instead of a password. Passkeys are tied to the domain you created
them on; if the instance moves to a new domain, add them again.

## API keys

Anyone can create API keys for the [REST API](/api-reference/introduction)
and the [MCP server](/mcp-server).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the key">
    Go to **Profile → API keys & AI agents**, enter a **New key name** (for
    example `Claude Desktop`), and click **Create key**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy it">
    Click **Copy API key**. It starts with `ch_live_` and is only shown once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A key acts as you, in the organization you were in when you created it,
with your role and project visibility. The list shows each key's first
characters, when it was created and when it was last used. Click the bin
icon to revoke one. Keys stop working when you're removed from the
organization.
