## Generating an invoice

From **Invoices → New**, pick a client. Consultainer shows everything for
that client currently eligible to bill:

- Unbilled, billable time entries (approved ones, if
  [timesheet approval](/concepts/time-tracking#timesheet-approval) applies —
  the page says how many hours are still waiting)
- Completed, unbilled milestones
- Approved, unbilled expenses

Select what to include, set the issue date, payment terms, tax rate, PO
number and notes, then generate a draft. Hourly time is grouped by project,
person and rate into line items; milestones and expenses each become their
own line.

## Payment terms

Due on receipt, Net 15/30/45/60/90, or Custom. A new invoice starts from the
most specific default: the **project's** terms when everything selected is
from one project that sets them, otherwise the **client's**, otherwise the
**organization's** (Settings → General, Net 30 unless changed). Changing the
terms recomputes the due date; Custom lets you set it yourself. The REST API
and MCP follow the same defaults when terms or a due date aren't given.

## Editing a draft

While an invoice is a **Draft**, its line items, dates, tax rate, payment
terms, PO number and notes are editable. Once it's sent, line items lock.
**Preview PDF** on the invoice shows it as the client will see it.

## Statuses

`Draft → Sent → Paid`, or `Void` before Paid. Marking an invoice paid can
record how (e.g. Wire, ACH, Check); online payments through Stripe or Mercury
mark it paid automatically.

## Billing cycles

A client can be invoiced on a schedule — weekly, every two weeks, monthly and
so on — from the **Billing cycle** card on the client page. Each run puts
everything unbilled for that client (billable time, completed milestones,
approved expenses dated before the run) on one invoice, using the cycle's
payment terms (which start from the client's). A run with nothing to bill
creates nothing. Invoices are left as drafts to review unless the cycle is set
to send automatically, which marks them sent. Cycles can be paused.

## Retainers

A **recurring invoice** (on the client page) bills a fixed amount on a
schedule — a monthly retainer, say — independent of logged time. Give it the
hours it covers to track billed-vs-logged hours on the client page. Like
billing cycles, it creates drafts unless set to send automatically.

## Sending invoices and tracking opens

**Email to client** (on an invoice) emails it to the client's contacts marked
*receives invoices* (or its billing email), with an optional message. Replies
go to you. The email links to the invoice's client page, where they can view
the PDF in the browser, download it, see how to pay, and pay online if Stripe or
Mercury is connected. Sending a draft marks it sent.

Prefer your own email? **Copy client link** gives you the same tracked link.

Consultainer records when the client opens the email, views the invoice, or
opens its PDF (including from a client or project share page), and shows it
under **Activity** on the invoice. The first open — and any open after a day
has passed — sends an **invoice opened** alert (Settings → Alerts). Opens by
people in your organization don't count. Email opens are best-effort: some mail
apps block the tracking image, and some privacy features load it for everyone.

## Overdue reminders

Under **Settings → Payments → Overdue reminders**, list the days overdue at
which clients get a reminder email (e.g. `3, 7, 14`). Each reminder goes out
once; turning reminders on doesn't send a backlog for invoices already past
several thresholds. A client can be opted out on its edit page.

## PDF and QuickBooks

Every invoice can be downloaded as a PDF. If QuickBooks is connected, an
invoice can be pushed to QuickBooks and its status synced back — see
[QuickBooks](/integrations/quickbooks).
