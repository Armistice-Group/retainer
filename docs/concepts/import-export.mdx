---
title: Import and export
description: Bring your history over from Harvest, Toggl Track or Clockify, and take all of your data with you
---

**Settings → Import** brings in time entries (and lists of clients or
projects) from Harvest, Toggl Track, Clockify or any other CSV.
**Settings → Export** downloads everything as a ZIP: one CSV and one JSON
file per kind of record, every uploaded file, and every invoice as a PDF.

| | Who can do it |
| --- | --- |
| Import, and undo an import | Owners and admins |
| Export everything | Owners |
| Export one client's records | Owners and admins |

## Import

### Export the file from your old tool

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Harvest">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the detailed time report">
        In Harvest, go to **Reports → Detailed time**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Pick what to include">
        Choose the timeframe (**All time** for your whole history) and leave
        the filters empty to include every client, project and person.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Export as CSV">
        Click **Export** and choose **CSV**. If your plan offers **Export →
        Custom**, keep at least Date, Client, Project, Task, Notes, Hours,
        Billable?, Invoiced?, First Name, Last Name and Billable Rate.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
    Harvest's time export has no email column, so people are matched by
    name. Harvest's project list export (Client, Project, Project Code, Start
    Date, End Date, Project Notes) also imports, as a list of projects.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Toggl Track">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the detailed report">
        In Toggl Track, go to **Reports → Detailed**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Pick the date range">
        Choose the range to bring over and clear any filters.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Download the CSV">
        Click the export (download) button and choose **Download CSV**.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
    The detailed report has User, Email, Client, Project, Task, Description,
    Billable, Start date, Duration and Amount columns. It has no rate, so the
    rate is worked out as amount ÷ hours. Durations can be `hh:mm:ss` or
    decimal.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Clockify">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the detailed report">
        In Clockify, go to **Reports → Detailed**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Pick the date range">
        Choose the range to bring over and clear any filters.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Export as CSV">
        Click **Export** and choose **Save as CSV**. Clockify's CSV export
        needs a paid plan (or a trial).
      </Step>
    </Steps>
    The hours come from **Duration (decimal)** (or **Duration (h)**), the
    rate from **Billable Rate**. Clockify writes dates in your profile's
    date format; the preview works out which (see [Dates](#dates)).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Any other CSV">
    Any CSV with a header row and, per row, a **date**, **hours** (or a
    duration, or a start and end time) and a **project** works. Commas or
    semicolons both work. On the preview you match each column to what it
    holds.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Files can be up to **20 MB** and **50,000 rows**. For more, export a shorter
date range and import the files one after another.

### How to import

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings → Import">
    You need to be an owner or admin.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the file and click Read file">
    Nothing is imported yet. The preview shows which tool the file came from
    and whether it holds time entries, projects or clients; change either if
    it guessed wrong.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the columns">
    Open **Columns** to see which column each value is read from. For a
    Harvest, Toggl Track or Clockify export they're filled in for you; for
    any other CSV, pick them. Time entries need at least **Date**, **Hours**
    (or **Duration**, or **Start time** and **End time**) and **Project**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match people">
    Under **People**, everyone in the file is matched to a member of your
    organization by email, then by name (a *by email* or *by name* badge
    shows how). Pick a member for anyone unmatched, or leave **Skip these
    rows**. **Assign everyone unmatched to** sets them all at once. If the
    file has no person column and you're the only member, everything is
    yours.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the preview">
    After changing anything, click **Update preview** to see the new counts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    Click **Import N time entries** (or **Import N clients and N
    projects**).
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Check it worked:** the page says *Imported N time entries (… h), N new
projects and N new clients*, and the import is listed under **Past imports**.
Open **Time → Team** for those weeks to see the entries.

### What gets created

- **Clients** are matched by name, ignoring case; a client that doesn't
  exist yet is created. Rows with no client go to a client called **No
  client**.
- **Projects** are matched by name under their client. New projects are
  hourly and not confidential.
- **People on projects:** everyone with time on a project is put on its
  team (if they aren't already), at the rate they used most in the file, or
  their usual bill rate if the file has none.
- **Time entries** get the row's date, hours (rounded to 0.01), notes,
  billable flag and rate:
  - The **task** goes in front of the notes: *Design: Homepage wireframes*.
  - **Billable** reads yes/no, true/false or 1/0; with no billable column,
    every entry is billable.
  - The **rate** is set on the entry itself (like an owner's rate
    override), so it's what the entry is invoiced at. With no rate, the
    project's rate for that person applies as usual.
  - If [timesheet approval](/concepts/time-tracking) is on, imported entries
    count as approved.
- A **projects** file creates the clients and projects only. Its rates are
  used only when you're the only member (you're put on each new project at
  that rate); otherwise set rates on each project's team.
- A **clients** file creates clients, with their email, address and phone
  if the file has them.

Rows the old tool had **already invoiced** (Harvest's *Invoiced?* column)
are imported as **non-billable** by default, so they're never billed twice.
Under **Rows already invoiced in the old tool** you can skip them or import
them as they are instead.

Amounts are imported as numbers. If the file is in another currency than
your organization's, the preview says so; nothing is converted.

### Dates

Dates are read as calendar days, never shifted by time zone: a row dated
March 2 is logged on March 2. `2026-03-31`, `03/31/2026`, `31/03/2026` and
`31.03.2026` all work. Whether the file is day-first or month-first is worked
out from the whole column; if every date would read either way, the preview
says *Every date in the file would read either way* and assumes month-first
(day-first for dotted dates). Change **Dates in the file are** if that's
wrong.

### Importing again

Importing the same file again only adds rows that aren't in yet: each row is
remembered, so the preview counts them under **Already imported (skipped)**.
Two identical rows in one file are both imported. If you delete an imported
entry, importing the file again brings that row back.

A row that changed in the old tool since the last import (a different date,
hours, notes, person, client, project, billable flag or rate) counts as a new
row, and the earlier version stays. To replace an earlier import with a
fresh export, undo the earlier import first.

### Undo an import

Under **Past imports**, click **Undo** on an import. It deletes the time
entries that import created, then the people it put on projects, and the
projects and clients it created, unless something else uses them now (later
time entries, invoices, documents, contacts and so on). Undoing doesn't
touch clients, projects or entries that were already there.

An import can't be undone once any of its entries is on an invoice: void or
delete that invoice first.

**Check it worked:** the import shows **Undone**, and the message says what
was removed and what was kept.

### Troubleshooting

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| *That file is over 20 MB.* / *That file has more than 50,000 rows.* | Export a shorter date range and import it in parts. |
| *That's not a CSV file. Export the report as CSV, not Excel or PDF.* | Export again and choose CSV. |
| *That doesn't look like a CSV with a header row.* | The first line must hold the column names. |
| *Pick the column that holds each entry's date.* (or the project, or hours) | Open **Columns** and choose it. |
| *Everything in this file has already been imported.* | Nothing to do: every row is already in. |
| *Nothing in this file can be imported. Check the rows with problems and the people mapping.* | Every row has a problem or belongs to a skipped person. |
| *N of this import's time entries is on an invoice. Void or delete that invoice first, then undo.* | Void or delete the invoice, then undo. |

Rows with problems are listed under **N rows can't be imported** with their
line number, for example *No project. Every time entry here needs one.*,
*"2026-02-30" isn't a date in the chosen format.*, *"abc" isn't a number of
hours.*, *Zero hours (a running or empty timer).* or *… hours in one entry —
more than a day.* Fix them in the file and import it again; rows already in
are skipped.

## Export

### Everything

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings → Export">
    You need to be the owner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Download everything">
    The ZIP is built as it downloads, so a large organization takes a while.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### One client

Useful when handing a client's records over.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings → Export">
    Owners and admins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the client and click Download ZIP" />
</Steps>

It holds that client and its contacts, links, projects, project teams,
milestones, tasks, comments, time entries, expenses, invoices (with lines,
events, payments and credit), estimates, agreements, documents, retainer
schedule and billing cycle, and the people who worked on it: their name,
email, role, employment type and title, but not their cost or default bill rate, bio
or résumé.

### What's in the ZIP

| Path | What |
| --- | --- |
| `README.txt` | What the export is and when it was made. |
| `data/<name>.csv`, `data/<name>.json` | One pair per kind of record: `organization`, `clients`, `contacts`, `links`, `projects`, `members`, `project-members`, `milestones`, `tasks`, `task-comments`, `time-entries`, `timesheets`, `expenses`, `invoices`, `invoice-line-items`, `invoice-events`, `payments`, `credit-notes`, `credit-applications`, `estimates`, `estimate-line-items`, `agreements`, `documents`, `recurring-invoices`, `billing-cycles`, `payment-methods`, `imports`, `audit-log`. |
| `files/documents/`, `files/receipts/`, `files/milestones/`, `files/agreements/`, `files/resumes/` | The uploaded files themselves. Each CSV's `exportedFile` column gives the path. |
| `invoices/<number>.pdf` | Every invoice, rendered as it looks today. |
| `manifest.json` | Row counts, and anything that couldn't be exported (`problems`). |

Records point at each other by id (`clientId`, `projectId`, `userId`, …).
Time entries also carry `project.name` and `user.name`, and members their
`user.name` and `user.email`. Money and hours are exact decimals, calendar
dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`, and times are UTC. The JSON files hold exact values;
in the CSV files, text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` gets a leading
`'` so a spreadsheet doesn't run it as a formula.

The timesheets, payment methods, imports and audit log, and résumés, are in
the whole-organization export only.

**Never included:** passwords, two-factor secrets and recovery codes, API
keys, share, invoice and estimate links, staffing-review links, integration
tokens and credentials (QuickBooks, Mercury, Linear, file services,
e-signature, SSO), calendar links and the Slack webhook. Values like these
are also blanked inside the audit log's recorded changes.

Every export is recorded in the [audit log](/concepts/alerts-and-audit):
*exported all of the organization's data* or *exported a client's records*.
Imports show as *imported data* and *undid an import*.

**Check it worked:** open the ZIP. `manifest.json` lists the row counts, and
`problems` is empty.

### Troubleshooting

- **The download stops part-way:** the archive is built while it streams; a
  timeout in a proxy in front of the app can cut a large one off. Raise the
  proxy's read timeout, or export one client at a time.
- **A file is missing from `files/`:** `manifest.json → problems` says
  which and why (for example, it couldn't be read from S3-compatible
  storage).
- **Only owners can export the whole organization.** Ask an owner, or export
  one client at a time as an admin.
