title: Calendars

Connect your calendar and the meetings you've had show up under **Time →
Meetings**, each with a suggested project. One click logs a meeting as time.

Calendars are personal: every member connects their own on their profile, and
only they see their meetings. Nothing needs setting up by an admin.

## Connect a calendar

You need your calendar's **secret iCal (.ics) link** — a private address that
lets Consultainer read the calendar. Find it with the steps for your calendar
app below, then:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Profile → Calendars">
    Click your name in the corner and choose **Profile**, then scroll to
    **Calendars**. (If you already have one connected, click **Connect a
    calendar**.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the link">
    Give it a **Name** (e.g. *Work calendar*) and paste the link into
    **Secret iCal link**. `https://` and `webcal://` links both work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Connect calendar">
    Consultainer checks that the link returns a calendar and pulls in your
    meetings from the last two weeks.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Check it worked:** you see *Calendar connected — recent meetings are under
Time → Meetings*, and the calendar is listed with *Synced (date) · every
hour*. You can connect up to five calendars.

Anyone with a secret link can read that calendar, so don't share it
elsewhere. Consultainer stores it encrypted.

### Google Calendar

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your calendar's settings">
    On [calendar.google.com](https://calendar.google.com), click the gear
    icon → **Settings**. Under **Settings for my calendars** on the left,
    click the calendar you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the secret address">
    Scroll to **Integrate calendar** and copy **Secret address in iCal
    format** (click the eye icon to reveal it, or the copy icon).
  </Step>
</Steps>

If there's no secret address, your Google Workspace admin has turned off
external calendar sharing; ask them to allow it.

### Outlook / Microsoft 365

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open shared calendar settings">
    In Outlook on the web, click the gear icon → **Calendar** → **Shared
    calendars**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish the calendar">
    Under **Publish a calendar**, choose the calendar and **Can view all
    details**, then click **Publish**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the ICS link">
    Copy the **ICS** link that appears (not the HTML one).
  </Step>
</Steps>

If **Publish a calendar** is missing, your Microsoft 365 admin has turned off
calendar publishing.

### Apple iCloud

<Steps>
  <Step title="Share the calendar publicly">
    In the Calendar app on a Mac, Control-click the calendar in the sidebar
    and choose **Share Calendar**, then tick **Public Calendar**. (On
    [icloud.com/calendar](https://www.icloud.com/calendar), click the share
    icon next to the calendar and turn on **Public Calendar**.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the link">
    Copy the `webcal://` link shown and paste it as is.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Fastmail

In Fastmail, open **Settings → Calendars**, open the calendar, and turn on
sharing it as a read-only iCalendar (.ics) link. Copy that link.

### Nextcloud

In the Nextcloud Calendar app, open the **⋯** menu next to the calendar and
create a **Share link**. From the link's menu, choose **Copy subscription
link**. Links that need a username and password aren't supported — use the
share link.

If your Nextcloud is on a private network, see
[private addresses](#calendars-on-a-private-network).

## Sort your meetings

Open **Time → Meetings**. Meetings are grouped by day, each with its time,
length, number of attendees and, when there is one, a suggested project and
why (e.g. *Attendees from acme.com (Acme)*). For each meeting:

- Pick or keep the project and click **Log** — adds the meeting's length as
  a time entry on that project, dated the meeting's day and described with
  its title. Untick **Billable** first if it isn't billable.
- Click **Ignore** if it isn't project work.
- Tick **Always for this series** (shown as **Same for this series (N)** when
  several are waiting) before clicking Log or Ignore to sort every waiting
  occurrence of a recurring meeting the same way, and future ones
  automatically on each sync. Future ones are logged billable or not the
  way you set **Billable** when you ticked it, and dated the meeting's day
  in the event's own time zone (or UTC, if the calendar doesn't give one).

When two or more meetings have suggestions, **Log all N suggested** logs them
all as billable in one go.

To stop sorting series automatically, click **Forget them all** on the
Calendars card on your profile.

Over MCP: `list_meetings`, `log_meeting`, `ignore_meeting`.

### Which meetings show up

Meetings from the last 14 days that have already ended, between 5 minutes and
10 hours long. Left out: all-day events, events marked *free*, cancelled
meetings, and meetings you declined. Meeting rooms and resources don't count
as attendees. An unsorted meeting that's deleted or cancelled in your
calendar disappears from the list.

Declines and "you" are recognized by the email address on your Consultainer
account. If your calendar uses a different address, declined meetings still
show up and you're counted as an attendee.

### How the project is suggested

From the active projects you can see, in this order:

1. The project you logged earlier meetings of the **same recurring series**
   to.
2. A **client whose email domain matches an attendee** — from the client's
   email, billing email, website and contacts (public domains like gmail.com,
   and your own team's domains, are ignored) — on the project you've logged
   to most recently for that client.
3. A **project or client named in the meeting title** (names of four or more
   characters).

## When it syncs

Every calendar is synced right after you connect it, then once an hour by the
scheduler. To sync one now, click its refresh icon on the Calendars card.
When there are meetings waiting, you get a notification at most about once a
day.

Removing a calendar (bin icon) deletes its unsorted meetings; time already
logged stays.

## Calendars on a private network

Consultainer fetches calendar links from your server, and only from public
addresses: `localhost`, private network ranges, link-local and cloud metadata
addresses, and `.local` / `.internal` host names are refused, including
through redirects or host names that resolve there. If your calendars are on
your own network (e.g. Nextcloud on your LAN), add this to `.env` and run
`docker compose up -d`:

```bash
ALLOW_PRIVATE_FETCH=true
```

This lets every member's calendar link reach your private network, so only
turn it on if you trust everyone who can sign in.

## Troubleshooting

Errors show on the add form, or in red under the calendar's name after a
sync.

- **"That link didn't return a calendar. Use the calendar's secret iCal (.ics)
  address."** — You pasted the calendar's web page or an embed link. Use the
  iCal/ICS link.
- **"Use an https:// (or webcal://) link."** / **"That isn't a valid
  link."** — Check you copied the whole link.
- **"Links with a username or password aren't supported."** — Use a share or
  publish link instead.
- **"That address isn't reachable from here."** — The link points to a
  private address. See [private network](#calendars-on-a-private-network).
- **"The calendar server answered 404."** (or 401, 403) — The secret link
  was reset or unpublished. Copy the new one, remove the calendar and add it
  again.
- **"Couldn't reach the calendar (…)."**, **"The calendar server took too
  long."** — The calendar server is down or unreachable from your server.
  It's retried every hour.
- **"The calendar is too large."** — Over 10 MB. Use a smaller calendar.
- **"You can connect up to 5 calendars."** — Remove one first.
- No meetings appear — Only meetings that have ended in the last two weeks
  are shown. Check the meeting isn't all-day, marked free, or declined.
